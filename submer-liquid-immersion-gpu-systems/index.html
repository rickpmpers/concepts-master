# **Submer Liquid Immersion GPU Systems: Architecture, Product Lineup & Deployment Insights**

*Prepared for Project SUN / Barn High-Performance GPU Infrastructure*

## **1. Executive Summary & Company Profile**

**Submer** (headquartered in Barcelona, Spain) is an industry leader in single-phase liquid immersion cooling (LIC) systems engineered specifically for enterprise artificial intelligence, high-performance computing (HPC), cloud hyperscalers, and rugged edge infrastructure. Submer designs "open-bath" single-phase immersion pods where server nodes, accelerator trays, and high-speed switches are submerged horizontally in a proprietary synthetic dielectric fluid termed **SmartCoolant**.

Submer's platform targets the fundamental thermal and operational bottlenecks of modern computing: standard air cooling becomes physically unsustainable when individual accelerator dies exceed 700W to 1,000W+ (e.g., NVIDIA H100, H200, B200 series). Submer delivers a verified **Power Usage Effectiveness (PUE) ≤ 1.03**, slashes cooling operational expenditures by up to 95%, reduces server component failure rates by up to 60%, and completely isolates computational hardware from harsh surrounding environments.

## **2. Core Technology: Single-Phase "Open-Bath" Architecture**

Unlike complex two-phase systems that rely on volatile fluorochemicals that boil into gas and require pressurized vapor-recovery seals, Submer's single-phase open-bath mechanism maintains the coolant in a continuous liquid state across the entire thermodynamic envelope:

  - **SmartCoolant Fluid Dynamics:** The immersion fluid is a clear, non-conductive, non-toxic, non-corrosive, and readily biodegradable synthetic hydrocarbon. It features zero Global Warming Potential (GWP = 0) and zero Ozone Depletion Potential (ODP).
  - **Natural & Forced Convection:** Fluid enters the bottom manifold of the immersion pod, flows across the compute surfaces (GPUs, CPUs, DIMMs, power delivery modules), absorbs thermal energy directly from heat sinks, and rises naturally to the top surface. Internal redundant pumps direct the heated fluid through an internal or external Coolant Distribution Unit (CDU).
  - **Secondary Loop Rejection:** Inside the CDU, a high-efficiency plate heat exchanger transfers heat from the SmartCoolant to an external water/glycol loop connected to dry coolers, adiabatic cooling towers, or facility heat-reuse networks.
  - **Server Fan Elimination:** By removing standard server chassis fans, server-level parasitic power draw drops by 10% to 15%, eliminating acoustic stress and vibration while freeing power capacity for actual compute cycles.

## **3. Submer Product Portfolio**

### **1. SmartPodX & SmartPodXL (Enterprise & High-Density Compute)**

The flagship SmartPod series represents Submer's modular datacenter building blocks designed for enterprise data halls, colocation hubs, and dedicated AI clusters:

  - **SmartPodX:** Designed to accommodate standard 19-inch IT gear or 21-inch Open Compute Project (OCP) form factors. Features up to 50 kW of continuous heat dissipation capacity across 21U / 19OU of submergible rack space. Integrated busbar systems deliver DC current (12Vdc / 48Vdc) with hot-swappable power shelves.
  - **SmartPodXL:** Designed for extreme-density AI/HPC clusters. Delivers up to **100 kW of heat dissipation** per tank across 45U / 42OU capacity. It can host dozens of high-wattage multi-GPU nodes in a single horizontal enclosure.
  - **Cool-Swap & Integrated CDU:** Features an in-tank or modular attached CDU, allowing service technicians to perform "cool swaps" of individual server sleds without halting loop circulation.
  - **Management & Telemetry:** Equipped with native Submer Cloud DCIM monitoring, redundant temperature/pressure sensors, automated top-cover actuation, and industrial API endpoints for central BMS/DCIM integration.

### **2. MicroPod (Compact & Rugged Edge Infrastructure)**

The MicroPod is Submer's self-contained, "datacenter-in-a-box" engineered explicitly for edge environments, remote installations, telco huts, and industrial or agricultural facilities:

  - **Compute Capacity & Dimensions:** Accommodates 6U of standard 19-inch or 21-inch OCP hardware with up to **7 kW** of continuous thermal dissipation in an ultra-compact footprint.
  - **Rugged Environmental Enclosure:** Engineered for non-datacenter spaces with IP65 / IP67 environmental sealing. The chassis is dust-proof, moisture-proof, and tamper-resistant, allowing deployment in dusty barns, outdoor huts, or unconditioned workshops.
  - **Plug-and-Play Cooling:** Includes integrated secondary cooling loops with flexible quick-disconnect dry-cooler couplings. Can operate with minimal facility retrofitting—requiring only power and network connectivity.

### **3. MegaPod (Modular Containerized AI Datacenter)**

  - **Scalable Prefabrication:** Housed within 10-foot, 20-foot, or 40-foot High Cube intermodal shipping containers.
  - **Capacity:** Scales from **100 kW to 800 kW+** of aggregate IT load using multiple integrated SmartPodXL tanks.
  - **Turnkey Infrastructure:** Pre-integrated with automated overhead gantry hoists, fire detection/suppression, internal fluid filtration, and exterior adiabatic or dry-cooler radiator arrays.

## **4. Server OEM Compatibility & GPU Integration**

Submer works closely with server vendors and the Open Compute Project (OCP) to qualify standard and immersion-born hardware platforms:

  - **Immersion-Born & Immersion-Ready Servers:** Standard platforms from Supermicro, Dell Technologies (PowerEdge XE series), Gigabyte, Wiwynn, and ASUS are certified for Submer pods.
  - **GPU Acceleration:** Compatible with dense multi-GPU compute trays featuring NVIDIA H100, H200, B200, L40S, and AMD Instinct MI300 series accelerators.
  - **Hardware Optimization Steps:**

1.  Removal of all internal chassis cooling fans and baffles.
2.  Replacement of non-sealed mechanical HDDs with enterprise NVMe solid-state drives.
3.  Application of immersion-compatible thermal interface materials (TIMs), such as phase-change materials or specialized thermal pads.
4.  BIOS fan-check override configurations to prevent firmware fan-speed error locks.

## **5. Case Studies & Real-World Deployments**

### **1. European Commission Joint Research Centre (JRC)**

  - **Context & Objectives:** The JRC required an ultra-dense, energy-efficient GPU supercomputing cluster for artificial intelligence and scientific research within a greenfield environment subject to strict CAPEX/OPEX caps and stringent sustainability goals.
  - **Deployment:** Installed Submer SmartPodXL immersion tanks supporting over 2 kW per compute node and exceeding 100 kW total rack compute density.
  - **Outcomes:** Achieved an audited PUE < 1.03, cut cooling energy overhead by over 90%, and demonstrated a 1-hour thermal battery effect (the thermal inertia of the SmartCoolant fluid buffer provides ride-through time during unexpected power or cooling loop dips).

### **2. Telefónica Edge & Harsh Environment Trials**

  - **Context & Objectives:** Global telecommunications provider Telefónica evaluated immersion cooling in hostile climatic conditions to assess equipment reliability at decentralized network edges.
  - **Deployment:** Submerged edge compute and packet-processing servers into Submer immersion enclosures exposed to elevated ambient temperatures and volatile humidity.
  - **Outcomes:** Confirmed absolute thermal stability across all silicon dies with zero throttling under full computational load. Eliminated dust ingress and component oxidation completely.

### **3. Radian Arc Cloud Gaming & Distributed AI**

  - **Context & Objectives:** Radian Arc required distributed edge infrastructure to stream low-latency cloud gaming and AI workloads globally across diverse telecom partner facilities without building custom raised-floor computer rooms.
  - **Deployment:** Standardized on Submer modular immersion pods housing high-density GPU accelerators across regional colocation hubs and edge stations.
  - **Outcomes:** Rapid plug-and-play rollout without modifying building HVAC, 25% to 40% reduction in Total Cost of Ownership (TCO), and prolonged hardware lifespan.

## **6. Practical Takeaways for a Barn / Solar / Battery Installation**

| System Dimension | Submer Deployment Strategy in an Edge / Barn Setup |
| :-: | :-: |
| **Sizing: MicroPod vs. SmartPodX** | For an initial deployment of a few multi-GPU servers (e.g., 2 to 4 servers drawing 3 kW – 7 kW), a **MicroPod (6U, 7 kW)** provides a fully contained, plug-and-play setup. If scaling toward multiple high-density multi-GPU chassis (drawing 15 kW – 50 kW), the **SmartPodX (21U, 50 kW)** offers full enterprise expansion headroom. |
| **Maximizing Solar & Battery Yield** | Because Submer systems operate with a PUE ≤ 1.03, virtually 97% of the solar and battery bank's kilowatt-hours feed the GPUs directly. Furthermore, removing server internal fans saves ~10% to 15% of server power draw, reducing the overall size and cost of the required inverter and battery bank. |
| **Barn Environmental Defense** | Agricultural environments suffer from sawdust, hay dust, moisture fluctuations, and rodents. Submer's sealed tanks completely seal sensitive electronics away from ambient air. The air inside the barn never touches the server motherboards or GPUs. |
| **Thermal Loop & Heat Rejection** | A simple, closed water/glycol loop runs from the Submer pod CDU to an exterior dry-cooler radiator mounted on the outside wall or shaded exterior of the barn. No water is evaporated, and heat is expelled directly into ambient air without requiring mechanical chillers. |
| **Thermal Battery Ride-Through** | The volume of SmartCoolant in the tank has high thermal inertia. If solar generation momentarily dips or the battery switches between discharge modes, the fluid absorbs heat for 30 to 60+ minutes before critical GPU thermal limits are approached, protecting hardware from sudden thermal shock. |
